Live AML monitor

A bank handles millions of payments a day, and a few of them are money laundering: criminals moving dirty money through accounts until it looks clean. This monitor checks every payment as it arrives, flags the suspicious ones, explains why, and groups connected alerts into cases.

Connecting to the monitor…

New here? How to read this page
  • The strip shows the last 24 hours of payments as grey bars. Each red dot is an alert that really was laundering; each grey ring is a false alarm.
  • Alerts are payments the model found suspicious enough to show an analyst. Click one to see why it was flagged.
  • Cases are alerts that share an account within 3 days. Click one to see the money drawn as a network of accounts.
  • The controls pause the replay, change its speed, and move the alert threshold, so you can see the trade-off every bank faces: more alerts catch more laundering, but waste more of an analyst’s time.
  • This replays four days of IBM’s simulated bank payments, so we know which payments really were laundering. A real bank wouldn’t know.

      How to read this page

      A bank handles millions of payments a day, and a few are money laundering: criminals moving dirty money through accounts until it looks clean. Banks must find it and report it. This monitor replays four days of IBM’s simulated bank payments and checks each one as it arrives.

      • The strip shows the last 24 hours of the replay: grey bars for payments, red dots for alerts that were laundering, grey rings for false alarms.
      • Alerts are payments suspicious enough to show an analyst. Click one to see why it was flagged, and the facts behind it.
      • Cases group alerts that share accounts. Click one for a suspicious activity summary, ready for a report, and a drawing of where the money went.
      • The model against the rules compares the model with the fixed rules banks have traditionally used, live.
      • The threshold is the trade-off every bank faces: lower it to catch more laundering, at the cost of more false alarms.

      The 8 shapes laundering takes

      Words on this page

      Score
      The model’s estimate, from 0 to 1, that a payment is laundering.
      Alert threshold
      The score at which a payment becomes an alert.
      Reason
      A plain-words explanation of an alert, such as “new payee”.
      Lift
      How many times more common laundering was among past payments with that reason.
      Case
      Connected alerts, investigated together.
      SAR
      A Suspicious Activity Report, which a bank files when it suspects laundering.
